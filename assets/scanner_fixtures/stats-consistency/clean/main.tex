\section{Results}

An independent-samples t-test revealed that group A scored significantly
higher than group B, $t(29) = 2.31$, $p = 0.028$, $d = 0.43$, 95\% CI [0.02, 0.44].

The standardized effect reached $z = 2.00$, $p = 0.046$.

The chi-square test of association was significant, $\chi^2(1) = 5.20$, $p = 0.023$.

A one-way ANOVA found a significant effect of condition,
$F(2, 57) = 3.21$, $p = 0.048$, $\eta^2 = 0.12$, $N = 60$.

\section{Additional analyses}

The correlation between the two measures was $r = 0.45$ with $n = 30$.

A Wilcoxon signed-rank test gave $W = 231$, $p = 0.084$; all pairwise
comparisons were corrected with the Benjamini-Hochberg FDR procedure.
